Conformational Design of Non-Fullerene Acceptors toward Low-Loss Organic Solar Cells

2026

Summer Research Student · Department of Chemistry, HKUST · Advisor: Prof. He Yan

Motivation Efficient organic solar cells must combine effective charge generation with low non-radiative energy losses. This project investigated conformational control of electron acceptors as a molecular design strategy for addressing this challenge, connecting intramolecular motion with solid-state emission and photovoltaic energy conversion.

Work Designed and synthesized tetraphenylethylene (TPE)-functionalized non-fullerene acceptors, comparing parent and ortho-methyl-substituted TPE units. The study focused on sterically restricting intramolecular rotation to modulate solid-state photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY). Literature-guided route planning, multistep synthesis, purification, and NMR characterization established the molecular basis for this comparison.

Significance Examined steric control of molecular motion as a strategy for regulating solid-state PLQY and reducing non-radiative voltage losses in organic solar cells. The project placed molecular synthesis within the broader challenge of controlling excited-state energy dissipation in photovoltaic materials.

Coordination-Directed Design of Hybrid Metal Halides for Photocatalysis

2025–2026

Undergraduate Researcher · Pb–Cu Project Lead · Tongji University · Advisor: Prof. Honghan Fei

Motivation Investigated the coordination-driven assembly of organic ligands and metal-halide building units into materials for light-driven chemical transformations. The project examined ligand choice, metal composition, and halide identity as design variables connecting crystal structure with optical response and photocatalytic function.

Work Synthesized a series of organic–inorganic coordination materials using different ligands and metal sources, focusing on Pb- and Cu-containing systems, including lead-halide coordination structures and molecule-connected Pb–Cu chalcohalides. Combined synthetic screening with diffraction-based structural comparison and optical characterization to examine composition-dependent material properties. Photocatalytic testing identified activity within this series.

Example In a Pb–Cu chalcohalide study targeting CO₂ photoreduction, developed a two-stage route combining Cu–thiolate precursor formation with solvothermal PbX₂ incorporation. Comparing Br/I analogues showed an absorption-edge shift from 546 to 557 nm and an optical-gap decrease from 2.41 to 2.35 eV, demonstrating halide-dependent optical tuning in this material system.

ZIF-67 Nanozymes: Synthesis and Kinetic Analysis of Enzyme-Mimetic Activity

Sep. 2024 – Jan. 2025

Project Leader · Tongji University Innovative Experiment Design

Motivation Investigated ZIF-67 as a coordination-based nanozyme, examining its glucose oxidase-like and peroxidase-like activities and quantifying its kinetic response to TMB.

Work Prepared approximately 200 nm polyhedral ZIF-67 particles by room-temperature coordination self-assembly; characterized their morphology and elemental distribution by SEM and elemental mapping; quantified enzyme-mimetic activity using UV–vis kinetic assays and Michaelis–Menten/Lineweaver–Burk analysis.

Evidence UV–vis kinetic analysis yielded an apparent Michaelis constant (Km,app) of 0.05 mM for TMB.
