About Me

I study how the structure of a photoactive material — the connectivity of a coordination framework, the conformation of a single molecule — decides what happens to an excited state after the photon is absorbed.

Three settings interest me for the same reason. In coordination-stabilized lead halide frameworks, a linker or a second metal can be used to fix the band edges without giving up chemical robustness. In organic solar cells, steric bulk around a rotating unit can suppress a non-radiative channel. In both cases the useful question is not what a structure looks like but which decay pathway it opens or closes, and my habit is to change one structural variable at a time and follow it through spectroscopy into function.

Publication

Fluorenone Acceptor–Linker Engineering of Lead Halide MOFs for Aerobic Photocatalytic Oxidative Coupling

Dongyang Li, Junkai Kang, Fanyu Meng, Wenyan Dan*, and Honghan Fei*

Inorganic Chemistry 2026, 65 (35), 20401–20410 · DOI: 10.1021/acs.inorgchem.6c02797

Coordination stabilizes a lead halide framework but the inert linkers that do the stabilizing tend to localize charge. An isoreticular pair — fluorenone-linked TMOF-24 against the carbonyl-free analogue TMOF-23 — isolates what an electron-accepting linker actually contributes: the absorption edge moves from 450 to 535 nm, the exciton binding energy falls from 59.98 to 39.22 meV, electron–phonon coupling weakens (Huang–Rhys factor 28.75 → 17.32) and the carrier lifetime lengthens. The payoff is photocatalytic — 99% imine yield within 2 h under simulated AM 1.5G for TMOF-24 against 32% for TMOF-23, with yields above 95% sustained over ten consecutive cycles.

My contribution: synthesis and structural characterization of both frameworks, and the benzylamine aerobic oxidative-coupling experiments.

Heterobimetallic Pb–Cu Chalcohalide Frameworks for Photocatalytic CO2 Reduction

Oct. 2025 – Present

Undergraduate Researcher · Project Lead · Tongji University · Advisor: Prof. Honghan Fei

Motivation Two-dimensional lead halide perovskites absorb strongly but their ionic lattices do not survive polar reaction media. Converting them into coordination frameworks fixes the stability problem while creating a new one: the inert ligands that hold the lattice together also localize charge and blunt visible-light response. The question here was whether a second transition metal and a conjugated thiolate ligand could stabilize the framework and modulate its band edges at the same time.

Approach I chose 2-mercaptonicotinate (H2mna), which offers thiolate S, pyridine N and carboxylate O donors in one ligand, and used hard–soft acid–base complementarity to place soft Cu at the sulfur sites while Pb binds carboxylate oxygen and the halide sublattice — an orthogonal coordination strategy meant to keep the two metals from collapsing into a mixed phase. Beyond one-pot solvothermal synthesis, I developed a two-step route that pre-forms a Cu–ligand precursor before introducing PbX2, which produced single crystals of both the bromide and iodide phases.

Result I collected and solved the SCXRD data for both structures and checked phase purity by PXRD. Tauc analysis gives optical gaps of 2.41 eV (Br) and 2.35 eV (I), with average TRPL lifetimes of 123.28 ns and 187.52 ns. Band structures and projected densities of states came from Quantum ESPRESSO. One experimental detail is worth flagging: acid has opposite effects in the two systems — it suppresses formation of the bromide phase but favours crystallization of the iodide phase, which shows how small shifts in protonation equilibria decide phase selectivity even between neighbouring halides.

Catalysis Under CO2 reduction conditions the iodide phase produced CO, C2H4 and C2H6; at the 3 h endpoint the C2 products accounted for 81.6% of the carbon among these three quantified products. The next step on my list is PLQY together with TRPL, which separates kr from knr, alongside XPS or bond-valence analysis to pin down the Cu valence.

Acceptor–Linker Engineering of Lead Halide MOFs for Aerobic Photocatalysis

Oct. 2025 – Jun. 2026

Undergraduate Researcher · Tongji University · Advisor: Prof. Honghan Fei

Motivation The same trade-off from the framework side: coordination makes a lead halide robust, but conventional inert linkers charge-localize and limit absorption. If the linker itself carried an electron acceptor, stability and excited-state behaviour would no longer have to be traded against each other.

Approach Synthesized and structurally characterized both frameworks — TMOF-24, built on the fluorenone dicarboxylate linker, and the carbonyl-free fluorene analogue TMOF-23 — an isoreticular pair that isolates the acceptor's electronic role instead of bundling it with a change of topology. I then ran the benzylamine aerobic oxidative-coupling experiments that test whether the optical change reaches function.

Result The acceptor shifts the absorption edge from 450 to 535 nm, lowers the exciton binding energy from 59.98 to 39.22 meV, weakens electron–phonon coupling (Huang–Rhys factor 28.75 → 17.32) and prolongs carrier lifetime. Photocatalytically TMOF-24 reaches 99% imine yield within 2 h under simulated AM 1.5G, against 32% for TMOF-23, and holds above 95% over ten consecutive cycles.

Isostructural Halide Series in TzPy–PbX2 Hybrid Crystals

2026

Undergraduate Researcher · Tongji University · Advisor: Prof. Honghan Fei

Motivation In the classic 4,4'-bipyridine–PbX2 family, swapping the halide also changes the framework topology, so the electronic effect of the halide is entangled with a jump in connectivity. A ligand that keeps the topology fixed across all three halides would separate the two variables cleanly.

Approach Using a thiazolothiazole-cored bipyridine ligand (TzPy), I synthesized and solved single crystals of TzPy–PbX2 for X = Cl (pale green), Br (yellow) and I (orange), recorded UV–vis diffuse reflectance spectra, and compiled the literature routes and PXRD screening for the 4,4'-bpy reference series.

Result All three TzPy analogues crystallize isostructurally in monoclinic P2/c, each containing one-dimensional edge-sharing [PbX2]n chains along b — whereas 4,4'-bpy–PbX2 switches from P-1 for the chloride to C2/m for the bromide and iodide. Holding the topology constant turns the series into a clean platform for asking what the halide alone does.

Ortho-Methyl TPE Acceptors for Low-Loss Organic Solar Cells

Jul. – Sep. 2026

Summer Research Student · Department of Chemistry, HKUST · Advisor: Prof. He Yan

Motivation In an organic solar cell the charge-transfer state at the donor/acceptor interface is weakly emissive, so non-radiative recombination outruns radiative decay and the energy leaves as heat. The group's charge-transfer/local-excited (CT–LE) strategy gives the CT exciton a thermally accessible route through an emissive local state. Tetraphenylethylene emits in the solid state, but phenyl rotation and low-frequency motion remain a non-radiative channel that the CT–LE route does not by itself close.

Approach Contributed to the design of ortho-methyl-substituted TPE acceptors, where methyl steric bulk is intended to lock phenyl rotation and lower the non-radiative rate at the conformational level. I then carried the multistep synthesis, purification and NMR characterization — including the dimethyl-TPE building block and the target acceptor — under Schlenk conditions with column chromatography.

Result Established a reproducible route to the methyl-substituted TPE building blocks and the target acceptor, with structures confirmed by NMR. The route initially stalled at a bromination step (CBr4/PPh3) and was traced to reagent quality; resolving that unblocked the downstream coupling.

Structure–Property Modeling of Two-Dimensional Hybrid Perovskite Optics

Oct. 2026 – Present

Independent Research Project · Tongji University

Motivation Reported optical properties of 2D hybrid perovskites are not consistent across sources: absorption peaks, absorption edges, photoluminescence peaks and band-gap estimates are used almost interchangeably, and a model trained on that mixture learns the labelling rather than the chemistry.

Approach Curated 168 structure–property records and audited each optical label against its source paper to separate those four endpoint types. Benchmarked a SOAP + ridge-regression baseline against composition-based and crystal-graph models under source- and structure-grouped cross-validation across 107 groups with fixed 5-outer/3-inner folds.

Result The crystal-graph models did not outperform the SOAP baseline on this dataset (MAE 0.2298 eV for SOAP + ridge, against 0.2908 and 0.2863 eV for the two graph models). The diagnostic result was the more useful one: at this data scale, label consistency and source separation dominate architecture choice.

ZIF-67 Nanozymes: Synthesis and Enzyme-Mimetic Kinetics

Sep. 2024 – Jan. 2025

Project Leader · Tongji University Innovative Experiment Design

Motivation A first project on coordination materials, asking how a porous framework can mimic an enzyme's catalytic machinery and how far that mimicry survives kinetic scrutiny.

Work Prepared approximately 200 nm polyhedral ZIF-67 particles by room-temperature coordination self-assembly, characterized morphology and elemental distribution by SEM–EDS, and evaluated glucose oxidase-like and peroxidase-like activity by UV–vis kinetics with Michaelis–Menten analysis.

Result An apparent Michaelis constant of 0.05 mM for TMB.
