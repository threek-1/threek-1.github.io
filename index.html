Research Experience

Design and Synthesis of TPE-Functionalized Non-Fullerene Acceptors for Solid-State Luminescence Control

Jul. 2026 – Present

Summer Research Student · Department of Chemistry, HKUST · Advisor: Prof. He Yan

Question Can methyl groups at the ortho positions of tetraphenylethylene (TPE) units restrict intramolecular rotation and tune the solid-state photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) of non-fullerene electron acceptors?

Work Designing and synthesizing parent and ortho-methyl-substituted TPE-functionalized acceptor candidates through literature-guided multistep synthesis, using chromatography for purification and NMR spectroscopy for structural characterization. The comparison is designed to test how methyl substitution affects solid-state emission.

Outlook Planned photophysical evaluation will test the proposed relationship between rotational restriction and PLQY, informing molecular design toward lower non-radiative losses in organic solar cells.

Halide-Dependent Structure and Optical Properties of Molecule-Connected Pb–Cu Chalcohalide Frameworks toward Photocatalytic CO₂ Reduction

Dec. 2025 – Present

Independent Undergraduate Project Lead · Tongji University · Advisor: Prof. Honghan Fei

Question How does halide identity (Br versus I) govern the structure and optical response of molecule-connected Pb–Cu–S–X frameworks being developed as candidates for photocatalytic CO₂ reduction?

Work Developed a two-stage synthesis based on 2-mercaptonicotinic acid that couples interfacial Cu–thiolate precursor formation with a solvothermal PbX₂ step. Optimized acid and halide conditions through PXRD-guided screening and compared the Br/I analogues using experimental PXRD, crystallographic models, and UV–vis absorption.

Evidence Replacing Br with I shifted the absorption edge from 546 to 557 nm and narrowed the optical band gap from 2.41 to 2.35 eV.

ZIF-67 Nanozymes: Synthesis and Kinetic Analysis of Enzyme-Mimetic Activity

Sep. 2024 – Jan. 2025

Project Leader · Tongji University Innovative Experiment Design

Question Can ZIF-67 exhibit glucose oxidase-like and peroxidase-like activity, and what kinetic parameters describe its response to TMB?

Work Prepared approximately 200 nm polyhedral ZIF-67 particles by room-temperature coordination self-assembly; characterized their morphology and elemental distribution by SEM and elemental mapping; quantified enzyme-mimetic activity using UV–vis kinetic assays and Michaelis–Menten/Lineweaver–Burk analysis.

Evidence UV–vis kinetic analysis yielded an apparent Michaelis constant (Km,app) of 0.05 mM for TMB.
